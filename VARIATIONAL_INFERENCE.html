Variational Inference (VI)

A smart approximation technique for intractable posteriors — powering VAEs, Bayesian deep learning, and scalable probabilistic AI.

📌 What is Variational Inference?

Variational Inference (VI) is a method to approximate intractable posterior distributions P(z|x) in probabilistic models. Instead of exact computation (often impossible in high dimensions), we choose a simpler family of distributions qφ(z|x) and optimize its parameters φ to make it as close as possible to the true posterior.

🎯 Key idea: Turn a sampling problem into an optimization problem. Minimize KL divergence between approximate and true posterior → equivalently maximize the ELBO.

💡 Motivating Example

Consider a latent variable model where posterior P(z|x) ∝ P(x|z)P(z). The denominator P(x)=∫ P(x|z)P(z)dz is often intractable. VI sidesteps this by approximating the posterior with a tractable q(z|x) (e.g., diagonal Gaussian) and optimising via ELBO.

log P(x) ≥ ELBO = 𝔼q[log P(x|z)] - KL(q(z)‖P(z))

🧬 Core Mechanism: Minimising KL Divergence with ELBO

Interactive demo: The true posterior (target) is a mixture of two Gaussians (intractable to compute exactly). Choose a simple Gaussian q(z) (mean μ, log‑variance logσ²) to approximate it. Adjust the sliders to minimise the KL divergence. The ELBO increases as the fit improves.

KL ≈ --
D_KL(q‖p) = ∫ q(z) log(q(z)/p(z)) dz   →   ELBO = log P(x) - D_KL

📉 Why KL(q‖p) is minimized

KL divergence is always ≥ 0, zero only when q = p. By adjusting φ we find the best approximation within the chosen family. VI trades off exactness for speed and scalability.

🔝 Role of ELBO

Since log P(x) = ELBO + D_KL(q‖p) and KL ≥ 0, ELBO is a lower bound. Maximising ELBO simultaneously improves the model evidence and reduces the KL to the true posterior.

🏛️ Variational Autoencoder (VAE) – The Iconic VI Architecture

Encoder (Inference network)
Takes input x, outputs parameters of qφ(z|x) (μ, σ). Reparameterisation trick samples z.
Decoder (Generative network)
Takes sampled z, outputs parameters of Pθ(x|z) (reconstruction).
VAE objective: ℒ = 𝔼q[log Pθ(x|z)] - D_KL(qφ(z|x) ‖ P(z))

Encoder learns to map data to a structured latent space; Decoder learns to generate realistic samples. The KL term regularises the latent space to a prior (usually N(0,I)).

🔧 How VI is used in other models

  • Bayesian Neural Networks – approximate posterior over weights for uncertainty.
  • Topic Models (LDA) – approximate posterior over topic proportions.
  • Diffusion models – some formulations use VI to learn reverse process.
  • Hierarchical VAEs – multiple layers of latent variables.

📘 Important Properties & Pitfalls

  • Amortized inference: parameters φ are shared across all data points (encoder network). Very fast at test time.
  • Mode‑covering vs mode‑seeking: Minimising D_KL(q‖p) forces q to cover all modes of p (can overestimate variance).
  • Local optima: ELBO is non‑convex; results depend on initialization.
  • Trade‑off: richer variational families (e.g., normalizing flows) improve approximation but increase complexity.

✅ Advantages of VI

  • Fast, scalable to large datasets (mini‑batch SGD)
  • Differentiable → end‑to‑end deep learning
  • Provides uncertainty estimates (latent variance)

⚠️ Limitations

  • Approximation may be too crude (e.g., unimodal q for multimodal posterior)
  • KL(q‖p) can underestimate uncertainty
  • Requires choice of variational family
⚡ Exam must‑know: VI turns Bayesian inference into optimization. ELBO = reconstruction likelihood − KL divergence. This is the objective of VAEs and many modern generative models.
